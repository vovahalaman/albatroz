# Confusion pairs

How to use: check the strongest evidence first. Text and plates beat architecture; architecture beats vegetation.
Never decide a pair from vegetation or landscape alone — these pairs were chosen because they look alike.

## Evidence ladder (strongest → weakest)
1. Readable text: language, street word, municipality name, phone prefix, web domain.
2. Licence plates (format and coloured bands).
3. Road signs: chevrons, stop-sign font, sign border, pedestrian-crossing stripe count.
4. Street furniture and brands: mailboxes, bins with a municipal name, ATM/telecom brands.
5. Road markings and bollards.
6. Architecture and building materials.
7. Vegetation, soil, terrain.
8. Sun position vs. timestamp (see "Time zone check") — strong but only when the sun is visible.

## Shared discriminators (apply to every PT ↔ ES and FR ↔ ES pair)
- **Street word:** PT "Rua", "Travessa", "Largo" · ES "Calle", "Avenida", "Plaza" · Galician "Rúa" · Catalan "Carrer", "Plaça" · Basque "kalea" · FR "Rue", "Place".
- **Letters:** PT has ç, ã, õ, "lh", "nh", "-ção" and no ñ · ES has ñ, no ç · Galician has ñ but no ç · Catalan has à è ò, "l·l", "i" = "and" · Basque has many k, z, tx, tz.
- **Municipality words:** PT "Câmara Municipal", "Junta de Freguesia" · ES "Ayuntamiento", Galician "Concello", Catalan "Ajuntament", Basque "Udala" · FR "Mairie", "Commune".
- **Phone prefixes (landlines):**
  - PT: 21 Lisboa, 22 Porto, 23x centre coast (234 Aveiro, 239 Coimbra), 24x Leiria/Santarém/Portalegre, 25x north-west (253 Braga, 258 Viana), 26x Setúbal/Évora, 27x interior north/centre (271 Guarda, 273 Bragança), 28x Algarve/Baixo Alentejo (289 Faro, 282 Portimão, 284 Beja), 291 Madeira, 292/295/296 Azores.
  - ES: 9xx — 91 Madrid, 93 Barcelona, 98x north-west/Asturias (981 A Coruña, 982 Lugo, 986 Pontevedra, 988 Ourense), 924/927 Extremadura, 95x Andalusia, 922/928 Canaries, 971 Balearics.
  - FR: 01 Paris area, 02 north-west, 03 north-east, 04 south-east incl. Corsica, 05 south-west. Mobiles (PT 9x, ES 6x/7x, FR 06/07) say nothing about region.
- **Domains:** .pt / .es (also .gal, .cat, .eus) / .fr.
- **Plates:** PT white with a yellow date band on the right on cars registered ~1998–2020 (newest AA-00-AA plates have no yellow band), formats 00-00-AA / 00-AA-00 / AA-00-AA · ES only the left blue band, format 0000 XXX · FR blue bands on BOTH sides, the right band has a region logo and département number.
- **Chevrons:** PT black with yellow arrows · ES black/white or blue/white · FR blue/white.
- **Signs:** ES red borders run to the sign edge with no white rim; PT and FR have a thin white rim. ES stop signs use a small font; PT stop signs a noticeably larger font.
- **Pedestrian crossings:** ES often 8 stripes (only Andorra matches). Count the stripes when a crossing is fully visible. [?]
- **Mailboxes:** PT red (CTT) · ES yellow (Correos) · FR yellow (La Poste).
- **ES-only signs:** "Coto privado de caza" / black-and-white hunting signs in rural areas; "Vado permanente" (Catalan "Gual permanent") on garage doors.
- **Road edge lines:** dashed outer edge lines occur on rural ES roads; almost never in PT.

## Time zone check (use with the Astrolabe tool)
- Mainland PT, Madeira: UTC+0 (summer UTC+1). Azores: UTC−1 (summer UTC+0). Mainland ES, Balearics, FR, Corsica: UTC+1 (summer UTC+2). Canaries: UTC+0 (summer UTC+1).
- At the same clock time the sun stands much "earlier" in Spain than in Portugal (about 1 h of solar time at the border, more further west in Galicia).
  Example: at 12:00 on the clock the sun in Madrid or Galicia is still clearly south-east of south (solar noon there is ~13:15–13:35 CET); in Lisboa it is much closer to due south (solar noon ~12:37 WET).
- Only valid if the timestamp is local time ([CONFIRM] with organizers) and the sun or a clear shadow direction is visible.

## North PT (Minho, Trás-os-Montes) ↔ Galicia
How to tell apart:
- Language first. Galician looks like Portuguese but uses ñ and no ç; "Rúa" (with accent), "Concello de …", "Xunta de Galicia" signs. Many Galician place names start with A/O/As/Os. Spanish-only signs are common in Galicia.
- Plates, chevrons, mailboxes and stop-sign font as above.
- Both have granite, eucalyptus, maize fields, stone crosses and raised granaries (espigueiro in PT / hórreo in Galicia); these do NOT separate the pair.
- Vineyards on high pergolas exist on both sides of the Minho river. [?]
- Sun/time-zone check works well here: Galicia is UTC+1 at almost the same longitude as Minho.

## Alentejo ↔ Extremadura (and western Andalusia)
How to tell apart:
- Both: flat or rolling montado/dehesa with cork and holm oaks, stone walls, cattle, pigs, white farmhouses.
- Alentejo: whitewashed low houses with a painted band (blue, yellow ochre) around doors, windows and the base; tall chimneys; Portuguese road signs with black/yellow chevrons.
- Extremadura: hunting signs ("coto", code "EX"), road kilometre markers with coloured borders and road codes (EX-, BA-, CC-), Spanish road edge lines, 8-stripe crossings. [?]
- Sun/time-zone check is the strongest tie-breaker away from towns.

## Algarve ↔ Andalusian coast (Huelva, Cádiz, Málaga)
How to tell apart:
- Algarve: ornate white chimneys ("chaminés algarvias"), flat roof terraces ("açoteias"), houses with coloured trim, calçada pavements, Portuguese signs; phone prefix 28x.
- Huelva: stone pines and eucalyptus on sandy soil, strawberry greenhouses [?]; Málaga coast: white houses stacked on mountain slopes.
- Andalusian towns: whitewashed walls with wrought-iron window grilles and flower pots — similar to Algarve; rely on text, plates and signs.

## French ↔ Spanish Basque Country
How to tell apart:
- Language order: in the Spanish Basque Country Basque is usually on top of bilingual signs (in Navarre Spanish on top), often with a thick black border. On the French side French dominates; Basque is secondary.
- Street word: "kalea"/"Calle" (ES) vs "Rue" (FR). Municipality: "Udala"/"Ayuntamiento" vs "Mairie".
- Architecture: French Basque (Labourd) houses are white with red or green painted timber and shutters, very uniform. Spanish Basque has more stone farmhouses (baserri) and high-rise apartment blocks in valleys. [?]
- Plates (two blue bands = FR) and mailboxes help in villages.

## Roussillon (Pyrénées-Orientales) ↔ Catalonia
How to tell apart:
- Both may show Catalan. In Spanish Catalonia Catalan dominates official signs ("Carrer", "Ajuntament", "Gual permanent"), Catalan flags with a star are common.
- In Roussillon official signs are French ("Rue", "Mairie") with Catalan only as a secondary name; French plates, French road signs, plane-tree avenues.
- Phone: FR 04 vs ES 972 (Girona) / 93 (Barcelona). [?]

## Madeira / Azores ↔ Canaries (and other islands)
How to tell apart:
- Language: Portuguese (Madeira, Azores) vs Spanish (Canaries).
- Azores: very green pasture with cows, black basalt stone walls and buildings with white plaster, hydrangea hedges, frequent cloud and rain.
- Madeira: extremely steep slopes, terraces (poios) with bananas and vines, laurel forest at altitude, levada channels.
- Canaries: mostly dry volcanic terrain, Canary pine at altitude, spurge shrubs and cacti in the lowlands; lush green only on north sides of Tenerife, La Palma and La Gomera.
- Time zones differ: Azores UTC−1, Madeira and Canaries UTC+0.

## Balearics ↔ Catalonia / Valencian coast
How to tell apart:
- Same language family (Catalan). Balearics: dry-stone walls, Aleppo pines, low sandstone houses with green shutters; island horizon (sea on several sides). [?]
- Phone 971 = Balearics.

## Corsica ↔ mainland south France / Sardinia-like scenery
How to tell apart:
- Bilingual French/Corsican place-name signs, often with the French name painted over.
- Mountainous granite with dense maquis shrubland right down to the sea; French plates and road signs.
- Phone 04 (shared with south-east France) — not decisive.

## Lisboa ↔ Porto (urban PT)
How to tell apart:
- Lisboa: white limestone calçada with black patterns, yellow trams, many azulejo façades, hills down to a wide estuary; phone 21.
- Porto: dark granite buildings and paving, steep streets to a narrow river gorge, azulejo churches; phone 22.
- Municipal bins and street furniture often carry the city name or coat of arms — look for it.
